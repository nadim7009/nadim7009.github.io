About

One question keeps coming back in my work.

Does a model that scores well on a clean benchmark still work on real data? I have asked it about plant disease photos, microscope images of microplastics, carbon-footprint survey data and soil sensors on farms. The answer is often "less than you'd think", and finding out why is the part I enjoy most.

I'm in the final semester of a B.Sc. in Software Engineering at Daffodil International University in Dhaka, with a major in data science. I work as a machine learning intern at FlyRank AI and I'm writing my thesis on causal uplift modelling.

Next, I want to do a master's or PhD abroad on reliable and explainable computer vision, ideally for agriculture or environmental monitoring. I'm also open to machine learning engineer roles.

Beyond clean accuracy: controlled robustness evaluation and corruption-aware training for plant disease classification

International Journal of Information Technology and Computer Science (IJITCS), MECS Press
Nadim Mahmud, Md. Siyam Hossain, Mehedi Hasan Riyad, Jannatul Jeor Bushra, Jul Jalal Al-Mamur Sayor

Plant disease classifiers report very high accuracy on PlantVillage, where every photo is clean. I tested four CNNs on nine corrupted versions of the test set (noise, low light and blur at three levels) and trained ResNet50 with those corruptions to see how much of the lost accuracy comes back. Grad-CAM and calibration checks show whether the models look at the lesions and whether their confidence can be trusted.

Attention-enhanced deep learning for microplastic segmentation in scanning electron microscopy images

International Conference on Advances in Artificial Intelligence (AAI 2026), Malaysia
Nadim Mahmud and co-authors

Counting and measuring microplastic particles in SEM images is slow by hand. I compared a U-Net with a four-gate Attention U-Net for pixel-level segmentation, removed the gates one at a time to see which ones matter, and tested both models under noise and changes in brightness and contrast.

Explainable machine learning for individual carbon-footprint prediction from traditional, digital and AI behaviours

International conference, 2026
Nadim Mahmud and co-authors

Most carbon-footprint models use housing, transport and diet. This paper adds digital habits and AI use to the picture. I compared XGBoost, an MLP and Random Forest in a leakage-safe pipeline and used SHAP to see which behaviours drive each prediction.

IrrigaSmart: predicting soil-moisture risk 12 hours ahead

Will this vegetable field drop below the irrigation threshold in the next 12 hours? I used open sensor, weather and field data from farms in Flanders, Belgium. Most of the work went into the data: removing faulty sensor readings, building a target that only looks forward, and splitting by crop cycle so the test fields never leak into training.

<0.003F1 drop, 30% sensor faults

A simple threshold rule reaches F1 0.972, so the model's gain is in recall: it misses 44 at-risk cases against 81 for the rule.

ThesisIn progress

Mitigating the correlation trap in email marketing with causal uplift modelling

A model that predicts who will buy is not the same as a model that predicts who will buy because of the email. Using the Hillstrom campaign data with injected confounding, I compare predictive models with causal uplift models to measure how often correlation-based targeting picks the wrong customers.
